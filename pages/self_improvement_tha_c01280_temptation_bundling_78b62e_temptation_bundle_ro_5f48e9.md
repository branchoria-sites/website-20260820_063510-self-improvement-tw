## Introduction

Temptation bundling can make a difficult beneficial activity easier to start by attaching an immediate pleasure to it. But the method has an important weak point: the bundle may depend on the very routine it is supposed to strengthen. In the original gym experiment, participants with gym-only access to tempting audiobooks initially exercised substantially more than controls, but the advantage declined with time and deteriorated particularly sharply around Thanksgiving, when the university gym closed and normal routines were interrupted.

That episode matters because it shows that a successful bundle is not necessarily a durable habit. When the enjoyable activity, the beneficial activity and the cues connecting them all depend on one place or schedule, a holiday, trip, illness or closure can remove the whole behavioural set-up at once. Evidence from [exercise]({{ 'exercise/' | relative_url }}) and habit research suggests that such interruptions can suppress behaviour even after ordinary life resumes.

## What happened after the Thanksgiving interruption

Katherine Milkman, Julia Minson and Kevin Volpp's original field experiment followed 226 university gym users. Participants in the strongest temptation-bundling condition chose four highly tempting audio novels and received them on an iPod that was stored at the gym. They could therefore continue their chosen stories only by returning to exercise. Another group received the audiobooks on their own devices and was encouraged to restrict listening to workouts, while a control group received an equivalently valued gift certificate.

The early result was striking. In the first week, the regression-adjusted gym-attendance advantage for the full-treatment group was 0.48 visits per week over the control group's estimated 0.94 visits — a 51% increase. Across the seven weeks before Thanksgiving, participants in the full treatment made an average of 7.79 gym visits, compared with 6.51 in the intermediate group and 6.11 among controls.

Then the calendar intervened. The university gym closed for Thanksgiving and students went home. The neat arrangement linking a particular place, an exercise session and exclusive access to an audiobook was temporarily impossible to perform. After the interruption, the earlier treatment advantage largely disappeared. In intervention week eight, average attendance was 0.75 visits in the full-treatment group versus 0.99 among controls; in week nine it was 0.47 versus 0.67. Those figures should not be read as proof that Thanksgiving reversed the treatment — the study was not designed to randomise people to receiving or avoiding the holiday — but they make the interruption an important failure point in the experiment.

The weakening had actually begun before the break. Across the nine-week study, the estimated full-treatment effect fell by about 0.07 gym visits per week, a statistically significant attenuation. Thanksgiving therefore should not be treated as the sole explanation for declining effectiveness. Rather, it appears to have accelerated or exposed a vulnerability that was already developing.

Milkman later described the interruption as a period of forced separation from the tempting stimulus: the effects had been robust for roughly seven weeks, but the Thanksgiving closure disrupted the pattern. That retrospective interpretation fits the observed data, while also highlighting the study's limitation: the holiday was a naturally occurring interruption, not a separately controlled experimental treatment.

## Why place-dependent bundles can be fragile

The Thanksgiving result becomes more understandable when temptation bundling is viewed as a cue-dependent system rather than simply a motivational trick. In the full treatment, several pieces lined up: go to the familiar gym, obtain the stored device, exercise and hear the next part of the story. Repetition could make those elements mutually reinforcing. But a closure removed both the opportunity to exercise in that setting and access to the exclusive temptation.

That is consistent with the broader science of behavioural routines. Habits develop partly through repeated behaviour in recurring contexts, so changes in location, timing and other [environmental cues]({{ 'useful-cues/' | relative_url }}) can weaken the connection between a situation and the usual response. Research on habit discontinuity has found that context changes can interrupt established behaviours precisely because familiar cues stop appearing in their normal configuration.

Exercise provides particularly relevant evidence. A study of 1,210 members of Danish fitness centres examined what happened around the Easter holiday. Immediately after Easter, gym attendance fell by 0.24 visits per week, or about 12.25% relative to the week before the holiday. The decline was especially pronounced among working-age members and people who had previously exercised at least twice a week. Although that study did not test temptation bundling, it strengthens the interpretation that holidays themselves can disrupt exercise routines rather than merely providing neutral pauses in them.

There is an important nuance, however. Context stability is not a simple switch that guarantees habit formation. An ecological momentary assessment study of 104 older adults found that more stable physical contexts were associated with greater moderate-to-vigorous [physical activity]({{ 'physical-capacity/' | relative_url }}), but its measures of context stability did not predict self-reported physical-activity habit strength. More recent longitudinal research has found context stability to be more consistently related to changes in habit strength, while also suggesting that the particular cue that matters can differ from person to person.

So the safest interpretation is not that changing place automatically destroys a temptation bundle. It is that a bundle concentrated around one configuration of cues has fewer routes by which it can continue when that configuration disappears.

There is another vulnerability specific to temptation bundling:**the temptation itself can become inaccessible at exactly the wrong moment**. Ordinary exercise habits may at least be transferable — someone whose gym closes can run outdoors or use another gym. In the original full-treatment condition, however, the novels were physically stored at the university gym. Exercising elsewhere during Thanksgiving could not reproduce the bundle. The commitment device that made cheating difficult also made the intervention less portable.

That creates a genuine design trade-off. Strong restrictions protect the exclusivity of the reward: if a gripping audiobook is available only during exercise, wanting the next chapter can create a reason to work out. Yet the tighter the restriction is tied to a particular venue or device, the more easily a disruption can make the whole arrangement unavailable. A looser rule is more portable, but also easier to break by consuming the temptation on the sofa.

## Designing pairings that can survive disrupted routines

The Thanksgiving episode suggests that the strongest practical bundle is not necessarily the one with the strictest possible restriction. It is one that preserves the crucial rule —*this pleasure accompanies this beneficial behaviour*— while allowing the behaviour to move between realistic circumstances.

For example, “I listen to this series only at my usual gym” is highly specific but brittle. “I listen to this series only while exercising” preserves exclusivity while allowing a hotel gym, outdoor walk, stationary bike or temporary alternative to keep the association alive. The appropriate degree of flexibility depends on the activity: broadening the rule so far that almost any situation qualifies would eventually remove the commitment value that makes the bundle useful.

Three design principles follow from the evidence:

* **Bind the reward to the behaviour more than to the venue.**Where practical, make the defining condition “while exercising”, “while doing household administration” or another beneficial action rather than “at this particular building”. The original experiment demonstrates the power of restriction, but also the risk of making access dependent on one location.
* **Decide on an interruption rule before the interruption.**Holidays, travel and closures are foreseeable. A predefined substitute — such as listening during a brisk holiday walk when the gym is unavailable — reduces the need to invent a new rule when familiar cues have already vanished. This follows from evidence that changes in context can disrupt the translation of established routines into behaviour.
* **Treat the first session back as important.**The Danish Easter study shows that exercise frequency can remain depressed immediately after a holiday rather than automatically snapping back to its previous level. Re-establishing the bundle promptly therefore matters more than assuming a pause is behaviourally harmless.

Later temptation-bundling evidence gives reason not to conclude that all such interventions inevitably fade after a few weeks. A much larger field experiment involving 6,792 commercial-gym members found that providing an audiobook together with encouragement to temptation bundle increased the probability of a weekly workout by roughly 10–14% and average weekly workouts by 10–12%, with effects detectable during and after the four-week intervention period. A subsequent exercise megastudy also found a significant intervention-period effect for a condition combining a free audiobook with an explanation of temptation bundling.

Those later results put the Thanksgiving failure in perspective. They do not establish that portable bundles are immune to routine breaks, nor do they erase the original decline. Instead, they show why the first experiment should be read as a warning about implementation rather than evidence that temptation bundling inherently stops working after several weeks.

## The lesson from the break

The Thanksgiving interruption exposes a distinction that matters for self-improvement:**a behaviour can be working because its supporting conditions are stable without yet being resilient when those conditions change**. Strong performance for several weeks is therefore not enough to show that a temptation bundle will survive a holiday, journey or altered schedule.

The original experiment provides an unusually clear illustration. Exclusive gym access to desirable audiobooks produced a large initial increase in attendance, but the effect weakened over time and was particularly disrupted around a holiday closure. Independent research on gym attendance and context change makes that pattern plausible: interruptions can remove the environmental and temporal cues that help repeated behaviours continue.

For temptation bundling, the practical target is therefore not merely a compelling pairing. It is a pairing with enough continuity to survive ordinary life. The enjoyable component should remain exclusive enough to make the beneficial activity attractive, but the rule should not depend unnecessarily on one building, device or perfect weekly timetable. A bundle that can bend when the routine changes has a better chance of remaining useful after the interruption ends.

        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
